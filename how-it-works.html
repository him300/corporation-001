Corporation 001 · mechanics

How the machine decides

Five participants. Each one is a TypeSafe System One model — a Jev — that answers exactly one atomic question per round and returns a distribution over three written criteria. Code, never the model, turns that distribution into an order size, a fill price, a spread width.

A 0.32 intent is a 60-share order, not a 32% one. Under 0.12 the dead zone holds — without it the book drowns in 10-share noise, and every round looks like a decision when it is only a shrug.

02 Replay — one bar at a time

The same five Jevs over a price path you bring. No loop, no look-ahead: at step i an agent sees bars[0..i] and nothing after. Their capital path, rebased to the first close, is drawn against the actual close.

Causality is structural, not a promise: the loop earns the previous decision before it asks the next question, so reordering it would break the accounting. Positions are notional fractions of capital, so the same engine runs on a R5 share and a 40 000 index.

03 Who decides what

The split is the whole design. The model supplies a judgement and nothing else; every number that can be wrong arithmetically is computed in code.

The model decides

  • Which of three written criteria the state most resembles
  • The shape of the distribution across them — summing to 1
  • A reported confidence — displayed, never used as a gate
  • That is all. It never sees a size, a price, or a fill

The code decides

  • The dead zone: |intent| < 0.12 is a hold
  • Order size, rounding, and the per-agent cap
  • Fill price, slippage, and who takes the other side
  • Impact, reversion, spread width, inventory skew, P&L

Consensus is a description of what five models think right now, not a forecast. They are careful readers role-playing a role — not traders trained on order flow.
